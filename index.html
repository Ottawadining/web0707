The Big Five

Only have a weekend? Do these five things and you can honestly tell everyone back home you've "done" Ottawa.

1

Visit Parliament Hill

Gothic towers, green copper roofs and free guided tours of where the country actually gets run. In summer, catch the Changing of the Guard on the lawn: bearskin hats, marching bands, the works.

2

Skate (or stroll) the Rideau Canal

A UNESCO World Heritage Site running right through downtown. In winter it becomes a 7.8 km skateway, and yes, locals really do skate to work.

3

Eat your way through the ByWard Market

One of Canada's oldest markets, packed with patios, produce stands and the BeaverTails stand that started it all.

4

Meet the giant spider

A three-storey bronze spider guards the National Gallery of Canada. Take the photo, then go inside for one of the country's best art collections.

5

Escape to Gatineau Park

Fifteen minutes from Parliament, you're in the wilderness. Hike to the impossibly green Pink Lake (it's not pink) and the Champlain Lookout.

🍽️ Eat & Drink

Come hungry. Leave happy.

From late-night shawarma to a tasting menu worth dressing up for, here's what you have to eat while you're here.

Eat a BeaverTail

Hand-stretched fried dough, shaped like a beaver's tail and buried in cinnamon sugar. The ByWard Market stand is the original, and even Barack Obama stopped for one in 2009.

Pro tip: Go classic cinnamon sugar first. You can get fancy on the second one.

Kid-approvedByWard Market

Join the shawarma cult

Ottawa takes its shawarma very seriously, with a shop on seemingly every corner. Order it "all dressed" with extra garlic sauce and don't make plans to kiss anyone.

Pro tip: It's the official after-midnight meal of the city.

Cheap eatsLate night

Cross a province for poutine

Fries, squeaky cheese curds, hot gravy. Hop the bridge to Gatineau and eat it in Quebec, where it was born.

Pro tip: If the curds don't squeak, it doesn't count.

Quebec sideKid-approved

Go big with a tasting menu

For one unforgettable night, book Atelier or Antheia. Both are run by winners of the Canadian Culinary Championships, and both are worth planning a trip around.

Pro tip: Book a few weeks ahead, especially for Friday and Saturday.

SplurgeDate night

Wander Little Italy

Preston Street is lined with trattorias, espresso bars and patios. Come hungry and pace yourself.

Pro tip: Save room for gelato. You'll want it.

PatiosNeighbourhood

Sip the local craft beer

The Ottawa–Gatineau region is full of small breweries and taprooms. Grab a flight and get chatting with the locals, who are nicer than you'd think for a city full of politicians.

Pro tip: Many taprooms have a food truck parked out front.

🏛️ Sights

Stuff you'll want a photo of

The capital does grand buildings and big views very well. Bring a charged phone.

Parliament Hill tours

Free guided tours of the House of Commons and the Senate, plus a lawn that fills up with picnics, yoga and protests (sometimes all at once).

Pro tip: Grab tour tickets early in the day. They go fast in summer.

FreeKid-approved

Watch the Ottawa Locks

A staircase of eight locks between Parliament and the Château Laurier, still cranked open by hand. Watching a boat climb the hill is weirdly hypnotic.

Pro tip: Summer afternoons are the busiest for boats.

FreeSummer

Major's Hill Park at sunset

The best view of Parliament in the city. Then walk across the Alexandra Bridge and look back from Quebec.

Pro tip: Golden hour here beats any postcard.

FreeViews

Rideau Hall

The Governor General's official residence, with huge grounds you can wander for free and trees planted by visiting royals and world leaders.

Pro tip: Play "spot the famous tree" on the grounds.

FreeHistory

Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica

Silver spires outside and a ceiling of deep blue and stars inside. It's the oldest church in Ottawa and genuinely jaw-dropping.

Pro tip: It's right by the National Gallery, so do both together.

FreeArchitecture

Rideau Falls

A waterfall that pours straight into the Ottawa River, a short walk from downtown. Loud, misty and free.

Pro tip: Spring melt makes it roar.

FreeKid-approved
🦕 Museums

So. Many. Museums.

Being the capital means getting the national museums. Rain in the forecast? Lucky you.

Canadian Museum of History

Just across the river in Gatineau. The Grand Hall of towering totem poles alone is worth the trip, and the view of Parliament from outside is a bonus.

Quebec sideKid-approved

Canadian Museum of Nature

A castle full of dinosaurs, a blue whale skeleton and live creepy-crawlies. Kids lose their minds. So do adults.

Kid-approved

Canadian War Museum

Moving, beautifully designed and full of tanks. Give it at least a couple of hours.

History

Science & Tech Museum

Giant locomotives, hands-on everything and a crazy kitchen that tilts under your feet. Built for touching.

Kid-approved

Aviation & Space Museum

A hangar full of planes from wooden biplanes to jets. Aviation nerds, this is your happy place.

Kid-approved

The Diefenbunker

A four-storey Cold War bunker buried in a hill in Carp, built to keep the government running through a nuclear war. Delightfully creepy.

Pro tip: About 30 minutes west of downtown, so you'll need a car.

🌲 Outdoors

Get outside, eh

Rivers, falls, forests and hundreds of kilometres of paths, most of them within a few minutes of downtown.

Bike the canal & river paths

Rent a bike and follow the Rideau Canal and Ottawa River pathways. It's flat, scenic and hard to get lost.

Kid-approvedSummer

Paddle Dow's Lake

Rent a canoe, kayak or paddleboat and float around. It's also the heart of the Tulip Festival in May.

Kid-approvedSummer

Hog's Back Falls

Churning rapids and rocky lookouts right in the city. Pack a picnic.

Free

Nordik Spa-Nature

An enormous Nordic spa in Chelsea: hot pools, cold plunges, saunas and a forest view. The ultimate reset, especially in winter.

Quebec sideTreat yourself

Raft the Ottawa River

Some of the best whitewater in North America is a short drive upriver. Scream, get soaked, repeat.

AdventureSummer

Beach day at Mooney's Bay

A sandy city beach on the Rideau River, with volleyball, lifeguards and ice cream. Nobody expects Ottawa to have beaches. It does.

🌷 Seasons

Ottawa does all four seasons

And it does them loudly. Here's the must-do for every time of year.

Winter

Dec – Mar
  • Skate the Rideau Canal Skateway
  • Winterlude in February: ice sculptures and snow slides
  • Beavertail on the ice (mandatory)
  • Thaw out at a Nordic spa

Spring

Apr – Jun
  • Canadian Tulip Festival in May, with a million blooms
  • Maple taffy at a sugar shack
  • Patios reopen and the city exhales
  • Rideau Falls at full roar

Summer

Jun – Aug
  • Canada Day on July 1 (wear red!)
  • Bluesfest in July
  • Changing of the Guard on the Hill
  • Paddle, bike and beach everything

Fall

Sep – Nov
  • Gatineau Park's fall colours, peaking late Sept to mid-Oct
  • Apple picking just outside the city
  • Museum days when it's chilly
  • Cozy dinners, big red wines

Fun fact: The tulips are a thank-you gift from the Netherlands. The Dutch royal family sheltered in Ottawa during WWII, and Princess Margriet was born here in a hospital room temporarily declared Dutch territory. Wild, right?

🚗 Day Trips

Worth the drive

Got a spare day and a car? These are all an easy trip from downtown.

Parc Omega

A drive-through safari park near Montebello where elk and deer stick their heads in your car window for carrots. Pure chaos. Pure joy.

Pro tip: Buy the big bag of carrots. Trust us.

Quebec sideKid-approved

Wakefield

An artsy riverside village in the Gatineau Hills with a red covered bridge, cafés and serious small-town charm.

Quebec sideScenic

Merrickville

A storybook village on the Rideau Canal, full of stone buildings, shops and working locks. Great for a lazy afternoon.

Scenic

Almonte

A pretty mill town with waterfalls in the middle of downtown. It's also the hometown of James Naismith, the guy who invented basketball.

🧭 Know Before You Go

The quick survival guide

🗣️

Bonjour, hi!

Ottawa is officially bilingual. Cross a bridge into Gatineau and you're in Quebec, where French comes first. A "merci" goes a long way.

🚶

Getting around

Downtown, the Market and most museums are walkable. The O-Train and buses cover the rest, and bike paths are everywhere in summer.

🧥

Dress for it

Summers are hot and sticky; winters are properly cold. Pack layers, and in winter a real hat. Locals will judge.

🌉

Two cities, one trip

Ottawa (Ontario) and Gatineau (Quebec) sit on opposite banks of the river. Many highlights are on the Quebec side, so don't skip it.
