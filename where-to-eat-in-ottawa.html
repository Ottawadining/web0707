🍽️ Come hungry

Where to eat in Ottawa. Seriously good.

Ottawa's dining scene has quietly become one of the most exciting in Canada. These are the tables worth building your trip around.

⭐ Our picks

The best restaurants in Ottawa

From nine-course tasting menus to ceviche a few blocks from Parliament, here's where we'd send a friend visiting for the first time.

Somerset West

Antheia

Chef-owner Briana Kim, a Canadian Culinary Championships winner, serves a nine-course vegetarian tasting menu built on fermentation. The flavours are so deep and savoury that you'll forget there's no meat. Think house-made sesame tofu with seaweed mole, or beets blackened in ancho paste.

It's one of Canada's 100 Best Restaurants, and one of the most original meals you'll have anywhere in the country.

Go for: A special night out, and to see what vegetables can really do. Don't skip the house-made non-alcoholic pairing.

Atelier

Ottawa's original modernist tasting menu, and still its most theatrical. Led by a Canadian Culinary Championships winner, Atelier serves a seven-course menu over about three hours where the line between cooking and art gets very blurry. Expect surprises on every plate.

Go for: Anniversaries, birthdays, or any night you want a meal to feel like an event.

Tasting menuBook ahead
📍 540 Rochester St.
🦪
Westboro

Supply and Demand

The neighbourhood restaurant every city wishes it had. Chef Steven Wall runs a lively food and raw bar with oysters, crudo, fresh pastas and shareable plates, all coming out of an open kitchen. It's warm, busy and consistently excellent.

Go for: Oysters and a glass of wine at the bar, and the famous fried Brussels sprouts.

Centretown

Gitanes

French cooking with a Montreal accent, served in a lovely historic building on Elgin Street. Chef Mitch Lacombe leans into classical technique, whole-animal butchery and bold, generous flavours. It's polished without being stuffy, and nobody leaves hungry.

Go for: A seat at the chef's counter if you can get one.

Tasting menuFrench
📍 361 Elgin St.

Downtown, at the NAC

1 Elgin

The National Arts Centre's restaurant sits right on the Rideau Canal, a short walk from Parliament. It serves seasonal, contemporary Canadian cooking with local ingredients, and it's the easy answer for dinner before a show.

Go for: A pre-show dinner, or a lunch break between sightseeing stops. Reservations recommended.

📍 1 Elgin St.
🔥
Wellington West

Stofa

"Stofa" is Norse for hearth, and that's the feeling here. Chef-owner Jason Sawision calls it approachable fine dining: creative, beautifully made food in a warm room that never takes itself too seriously. Choose three courses à la carte, or let the kitchen add two more.

Go for: A fine-dining meal that feels like dinner at a very talented friend's house.

Downtown, Elgin Street

Raphaël Peruvian Cuisine

Chef Lizardo Becerra brings Peru's coast, mountains and jungle to Elgin Street in a lush, green dining room. Start with the ceviche, then move on to dishes like ají panca–marinated suckling pig. It's refined, bright and full of flavour you won't find anywhere else in the city.

Go for: Ceviche and a pisco sour, just a few blocks from Parliament.

🧭 Dining tips

Good to know

📅

Book ahead

The tasting-menu spots are small and fill up, especially on Fridays and Saturdays. Reserve a few weeks before your trip.

🗓️

Check the days

Many of Ottawa's best restaurants are closed Sundays and Mondays, and some only open a few nights a week. Plan your big dinner around them.

🥕

Tell them early

Tasting menus are hard to change on the fly. Mention any allergies or dietary needs when you book, not when you sit down.
